KAIRÓS Internals

Everything behind the KPI intelligence-to-action engine: the thesis it encodes, the seven-stage pipeline, the governance that decides what it is allowed to say, and the toolkit choices with the reasoning kept.

01The thesis

Dashboards detect. They do not explain. The distance between a number moving and a person acting on it is measured in days, and that distance is where the value sits.

The product is an explanation layer that sits on top of the BI stack a client already owns. It does not replace the warehouse, the semantic layer, or Power BI. It attaches to them and answers the question they structurally cannot: why did this move, how sure are we, and what should someone do about it.

The one architectural commitment

Everything in the build follows from a single reading of the evidence on what language models are and are not good at:

Design commitment

Large language models are strong at proposing causal explanations from world knowledge and near-random at inferring causation from correlation. So the model proposes and phrases. It never decides, and it never computes.

Kıcıman et al. (TMLR 2024) found LLMs beat existing algorithms on knowledge-based causal tasks — 97% on pairwise causal discovery. The Corr2Cause benchmark (ICLR 2024) tested seventeen models on inferring causation from correlation and found them near random, with fine-tuned versions failing out of distribution. Both are true. The architecture is a direct read of where the split falls.

That commitment is enforced by code at runtime, not promised in a diagram. Two mechanisms do the enforcing — the closed evidence packet and the numeric guard — and both are described in section 04. The observable consequence: a full run reports 91–100% of its reasoning steps as non-LLM, and the engine produces a complete, correct answer with the model switched off entirely.

What it refuses to build

Cortex Analyst, Databricks Genie and BigQuery Gemini were all generally available by mid-2026. Text-to-SQL is a platform feature now, not a product. The engine calls those layers rather than competing with them, and owns the three things platforms do not ship: a standard of proof, evidence that lives outside the warehouse, and memory of what worked last time.

03The seven stages

Each stage is defined as much by what it refuses to do as by what it computes. The refusals are the product.

FITNESSfitness.py
Is this estate fit to be reasoned over at all?
Five dimensions — availability, timeliness, completeness, consistency, validity — across every source the run touches. Cross-source checks catch shipments referencing orders that do not exist and shipments dated before their own order. Returns FIT, FIT_WITH_CAVEATS or UNFIT.
Refuses to analyse an estate that fails the gate. UNFIT halts the run.
RULESSQL
SIFTsift.py
Is the change real, and does it matter?
Median-based weekly seasonal index so the anomaly cannot contaminate the baseline built to detect it. Robust sigma from the MAD of pre-window residuals. Then a materiality gate: rupee floor, share of period plan, and persistence in days.
Refuses to wake anyone for a statistically real but immaterial move.
STATISTICSRULESSQL
SPLITsplit.py
Where exactly did it happen?
Revenue = volume × price is an identity, so the movement decomposes exactly into volume, mix and rate with no model. Separately, a dimension-lattice scan ranked by explanatory power weighted by share shift, following Adtributor (NSDI 2014).
Refuses to leave a residual. The identity closes to under ₹1.
DETERMINISTICSQL
SOURCEevidence.py
Why, and how sure are we?
Rule-based hypothesis generation from the contribution scan, the causal graph and the event calendar. Each candidate is tested for graph admissibility, temporal precedence against the declared lag, dose–response gradient, corroboration with conflict scoring, and a difference-in-differences with a placebo test.
Refuses to award L3 when the placebo fails, and refuses any hypothesis with no admissible mechanism.
CAUSALSTATISTICSRETRIEVALSQL
PROPAGATEpropagation.py
Where in the chain is the loss created?
Walks the mechanism path and measures every hop with a governed KPI against the same untreated cohort — each in a window shifted back by that hop's cumulative declared lag. Latent nodes are named, not skipped. Trailing metrics report how much of their window is actually post-onset.
Refuses to measure an upstream cause in its effect's window.
CAUSALSQL
SOLVEsolve.py
What should someone actually do?
Recommendations are retrieved from playbook memory — past interventions with measured outcomes — matched on pattern signature and rescaled to the value now at risk. Then routed to a human–AI collaboration mode by delegation.py.
Refuses to emit an action whose lever is not declared in the contract, and refuses to dress a generic suggestion as institutional memory when similarity is below 0.45.
RETRIEVALRULES
NARRATEnarrate.py
How do we say it in this reader's language?
The model receives a closed JSON evidence packet of already-computed facts, with no tools and no data access. Column entitlements are applied to the packet itself, so a denied figure never reaches the prompt. Afterwards every number in the output is parsed and matched against the packet.
Refuses to publish a number that is not in the packet. Fails, retries once, then falls back to the deterministic narrator.
LLMDETERMINISTIC
Why the order matters

Fitness runs before SIFT because a stale or partial feed produces movements that look exactly like business events. Propagate runs after SOURCE because it needs the confirmed mechanism path. Narrate runs last and receives nothing but conclusions — it cannot reach back into the data even if the prompt asked it to.

04Four governors

These are the mechanisms that decide what the engine is permitted to say. They are the difference between this and an insight generator.

The Evidence Ladder

Bradford Hill's nine viewpoints (1965) operationalised on Pearl's causal hierarchy (CACM, 2019). Hill was explicit that his viewpoints are not a checklist and none is a sine qua non — which is the argument for a graded ladder rather than a pass/fail causality test. Nothing below L2 is ever phrased as a cause.

L0
Co-movement
Two series moved together. Never stated as a cause.
language permitted: “moved alongside”
L1
Precedence and gradient
The cause began before the effect, and the gap matches the lag the causal graph declares for that mechanism. Optionally reinforced by a monotone dose–response gradient across exposure buckets.
language permitted: “associated with”
L2
Independent corroboration
Unstructured evidence from sources that do not share a pipeline agrees — at least three on-theme documents, two independent source types, two distinct accounts — and conflicting evidence is below 40%.
language permitted: “likely cause”
L3
Counterfactual
Difference-in-differences against a real untreated cohort, estimated on the log-ratio series so common shocks difference out, with a placebo test on the pre-period that must validate before the rung is awarded.
language permitted: “quantified at −13.4%, p<0.0001”
A subtlety worth knowing

The ladder is not strictly cumulative. L3 does not require L2, because a validated counterfactual is stronger evidence than corroborating text and needs no text at all. This matters in practice: the CFO persona is denied CRM verbatims, so it can never reach L2 — but it still reaches L3 on structured evidence that was sitting right there.

The Ambiguity Protocol

When explanations tie, the engine returns Confirmed / Competing / Unknown, shows the rivals side by side, and prices the cheapest test that separates them. Ambiguity becomes a next step rather than a hedge. Showing rival hypotheses by default is a cognitive forcing function — Buçinca et al. (CSCW 2021) found these measurably reduce over-reliance on incorrect AI suggestions.

The Numeric Guard

Post-generation, every number in the narrative is parsed and matched against the evidence packet within a 1% tolerance that survives legitimate rounding. Unverifiable figures fail the narrative, which is retried once and then replaced by the deterministic narrator.

narrate.py — the guard in practice
numeric_guard("Revenue fell Rs 1.85 Cr, down 16.1%.", packet)
→ (True,  [])

numeric_guard("Revenue fell Rs 4.2 Cr, margin lost 9.7%.", packet)
→ (False, ['4.2'])          # ₹4.2 Cr is absent from the packet → narrative fails

The domain causal graph

Twelve nodes, twelve edges, four explicit blocks. Its only job is to reject hypotheses with no defensible mechanism before they are scored or shown. The blocks exist because a fluent model will happily invent the connection: weather → reorder behaviour, competitor promotion → our own dispatch performance. Each edge carries a lag in days, and that lag is load-bearing — it is what the precedence test checks against and what the mechanism ledger aligns its measurement windows to.

05The contract layer

Five YAML files, 399 lines. This is the surface a client tunes, and the reason the engine is portable across sectors without touching Python.

FileLinesCarries
kpi_contract.yaml190Source definitions with grain, refresh cadence and freshness SLA. Five KPIs with SQL, additivity, drivers, materiality thresholds, sliceable dimensions, lineage and access class. Six business levers with owner, decision right, authority limit, reversibility and external-facing flag.
causal_graph.yaml47Nodes with observables and type (operational, latent, behavioural, commercial, external, instrumentation). Edges with sign, lag and a defensible mechanism sentence. Explicit blocks with reasons.
entitlements.yaml70Four personas with row filters, denied columns, denied evidence domains, PII policy, and narrative shape — depth, focus, word limit, delivery channel.
playbooks.yaml79Three seeded interventions with pattern signature, actions, and measured outcome including recovery rate, weeks to effect, cost and the measurement method used.
llm_pricing.yaml13Per-model token prices, FX rate, and the routing policy that maps job type to the cheapest model clearing the bar.

Two rules the contract enforces absolutely

  • An undeclared KPI cannot be computed. get_kpi() raises on anything not in the contract. This is what stops a model inventing a metric definition.
  • An undeclared lever cannot be recommended. An action whose lever is not in the contract is dropped before emission, which is what keeps recommendations inside real decision rights.
Recent change

Decision rights used to be prose — "RSM up to Rs 25L; above that CFO approval" — that the engine could print but never check. Levers now carry authority_limit_inr, reversibility and external_facing, which makes the delegation router possible.

Known property of synthetic data

Effect sizes are recovered because they were planted. On real client data the honest expectation is more L1 and L2 and considerably fewer L3s — a validated counterfactual needs a genuinely untreated cohort, and often one does not exist.

07Security model

Entitlements are applied to the data and the evidence set before anything reaches a prompt. Post-filtering generated text is not a control an auditor will accept — a model that has seen a number will leak it.

PersonaRowColumnDomainResult on S1
CFOall regions—no CRM verbatimsL3 via counterfactual; 23 evidence items withheld
RSM Northregion = Northno margin—L3; 4 items withheld; refused outright on a West query
Supply Chainall regionsno ₹ at allno verbatimsMovement shown as % only; packet holds no currency figure
Analystall regions——Full method detail and lineage

Three enforcement points

  1. Pre-flight refusal. A persona whose row entitlement does not intersect the requested scope is refused before the query runs. We never execute and return an empty result, because “no data” and “not allowed to see this data” are different answers and conflating them discloses what exists.
  2. SQL compilation. Row filters are compiled into the WHERE clause, so restricted rows never enter a dataframe.
  3. Packet redaction. Denied measures are replaced in the evidence packet itself, so the value cannot reach the model. A test asserts the Supply Chain packet contains no rupee figure anywhere in its JSON.
An honest result kept rather than hidden

A narrower entitlement can produce genuinely weaker evidence: a row filter shrinks the untreated cohort available for a counterfactual. When that happens the engine raises an explicit advisory saying so, rather than silently returning a lower grade.

08Estate operations

Two capabilities that answer the questions a monitoring product is always asked and rarely answers with a number.

Sweep — what should I look at today?

Scans every governed KPI across every slice, applies the materiality gate, and reports not only what survived but how much was suppressed and under which gate. Alert fatigue is treated as a measurable property.

The sweep also shrinks with entitlement: the analyst scans 73 slices, the North RSM 58. And grain mismatches are reported, not raised — a shipment-grain KPI genuinely cannot be cut by customer segment, and that is a fact about the estate worth surfacing.

09Learning loop

Three things happen when an analyst grades a narrative or an outcome lands.

  1. The grade is stored as a labelled example, so the evaluation set grows itself.
  2. A rejected hypothesis is down-weighted. Priors multiply into the leader ranking, so a repeatedly-rejected explanation sinks. Weights are clamped to [0.35, 1.6] so feedback cannot drive a hypothesis to zero or dominance.
  3. A measured outcome re-estimates the playbook's effect size as a weighted mean over observations, so recommendations are calibrated by this company's own track record.

State lives in runtime/ and is gitignored. cli.py --reset and POST /api/reset clear it, because a system that learns needs an explicit way to forget and demos must start from a known point.

Scope of the claim

The learning is shallow and should be described that way. Priors re-weight ranking and outcomes re-estimate effect sizes. Nothing is fine-tuned, and no model weights change.

Deliberate non-choices

  • No vector database. BM25 over a segment- and window-scoped slice reads a few hundred documents per insight rather than embedding a corpus. This is what keeps evidence retrieval affordable per insight. At production corpus sizes this would need a vector index — stated as a limitation, not hidden.
  • No ORM. All SQL is generated from the semantic contract. Model-authored SQL is never executed.
  • No frontend framework and no build step. 386 lines of vanilla JS, inline SVG charts, no CDN. The UI is a demonstration surface, and a build toolchain would be one more thing for a reviewer to get working.
  • No orchestration framework. The pipeline is a function with seven context-managed stages. LangChain-style abstraction would obscure exactly the control flow that is the product's argument.

15Limitations

Stated plainly, because the entire thesis is about not overclaiming. These belong in the Q&A, not hidden.

  • Synthetic data. Effect sizes are recovered because they were planted.
  • DiD needs a real control. Where every unit is treated, the engine correctly returns L1 and abstains. This is common in practice.
  • The causal graph is hand-curated. That is the point — it encodes domain judgement — but it is maintenance, and a wrong edge silently blocks a true cause.
  • BM25, not embeddings. Fine at this scale; needs a vector index at production corpus sizes.
  • Three seeded playbooks. The asset compounds only with use.
  • The numeric guard is magnitude-tolerant. It matches within 1% to survive rounding, so a fabricated figure landing within 1% of a real one can pass. It is a strong filter on material fabrication, not a proof of correctness.
  • Personalisation is rated low by the field. Olszak & Bartuś found decision personalisation the least-cited benefit (4 of 20) — worth knowing, since persona-shaping is a large part of this design. Read as under-delivered rather than unwanted.

16UX: current state and open questions

The UI was built as a demonstration surface for the engine, not as a designed product. It works and every control is wired, but it is the least considered part of the build — which is where your direction comes in.

What exists today

A single dark page, two columns, eleven cards, 386 lines of vanilla JS. Three selectors drive everything — scenario, persona, narrator — and every state is a URL. Cards render in a fixed order regardless of what the run actually found.

Known weaknesses I would fix regardless

Everything has equal visual weightopen
Eleven cards in a fixed order. Nothing tells the eye that the verdict and the recommendation matter more than the telemetry. A CFO and an analyst see the same layout with different words in it, when they should probably see different shapes.
The series chart is dense and unlabelledopen
Weekly seasonality makes it a picket fence. The band and break markers are correct but hard to read, there is no y-axis, and the window under analysis is not marked against the baseline.
No route into the estate viewopen
Sweep and backtest are among the strongest capabilities and exist only as CLI and API. A reviewer never sees them unless told to open a terminal.
Feedback is a dead endopen
Accept and reject write to the store and print a prior weight, but nothing shows the analyst what changed or lets them enter a correction. The outcome endpoint has no UI at all.
Abstention looks like failureopen
S2 is one of the best things the engine does, and it currently renders as an amber badge and a wall of text. The separating test — a priced, owned, dated experiment — should probably be the most prominent element on that screen.

Decisions I need from you

Who is the primary audience of the screen?
Right now it serves the analyst best, because it shows everything. If the judge-facing story is the executive, the default view should probably lead with narrative and recommendation and put method behind a disclosure.
One screen or a flow?
A single dense dashboard reads as a product. A guided sequence — movement → evidence → action — reads better in a two-minute demo and in the video. These pull in different directions.
How much method should be visible by default?
The method ledger is the proof of the central claim, and it is also the most intimidating thing on the page. Always visible, behind a toggle, or a separate view?
Does the estate view become the landing screen?
“Here are 73 slices, 24 material, here is why the rest were suppressed” is a strong opening that frames every scenario as one item from a worklist. It is also a second screen to design and build.
Keep the dark theme?
It reads as a technical instrument, which suits the argument. A light theme would read more like enterprise BI and sit closer to the Accenture deck. Currently dark-only.
Sequencing

The video has not been re-recorded and will not be until the UX is settled, so the final walkthrough reflects the finished interface rather than this one.

17Running it

From a clean clone
./run.sh                                   # installs, builds the estate, serves :8000
python cli.py --scenario S1 --persona cfo  # headless single run
python cli.py --all                        # every scenario × persona
python cli.py --sweep --backtest           # estate operations
python cli.py --reset                      # clear learned state
python -m pytest tests/ -q                 # 47 tests against planted ground truth
python data/generate.py                    # rebuild the estate (seeded, reproducible)
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-...            # optional: enables live narration
